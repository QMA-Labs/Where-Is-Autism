## 01 / The unit of observation

**Foundation · Conceptual premise**

> What is lost when behaviour is interpreted without its measurement context?

**Originating observation.** Masiha’s starting concern is that a visible response can become the whole account of a person, while the conditions under which that response occurred remain unspecified.

**Interpretation.** Treat the observation as an event involving a person, a state, a setting and a response demand. This widens the information considered; it does not establish that every factor matters in every case.

**What to measure.** Define the target outcome first. Record task instructions, response format, context and timing separately from person-level characteristics.

**Competing explanation.** Adding contextual variables can create complexity without improving explanation. Genuine impairments and support needs must not disappear from the account.

**A possible test.** Compare an explicitly defined baseline with a context-enriched model on observations from held-out people. State the minimum useful improvement before analysis.

**Evidence boundary.** The broad framework is a proposed organization of questions, not a new definition or established theory of autism.

**Selected sources:** [World Health Organization (2025)](REFERENCES.md#who) · [NICE (CG170)](REFERENCES.md#nice)

## 02 / Ongoing thought

**State & attention · Related empirical work**

> Can internal experience vary independently of how much response an observer sees?

**Originating observation.** Masiha interpreted some periods of absorption or limited response as potentially involving sustained internal activity. This is an interpretation, not a participant’s verified account.

**Interpretation.** Keep three records separate: observable behaviour, the person’s own report, and the observer’s inference. Limited response does not identify the content or complexity of thought.

**What to measure.** Accessible experience sampling during a defined task, paired with task performance and context. Evaluate whether the reporting method is suitable for the participant.

**Competing explanation.** Fatigue, misunderstanding, interest, anxiety or difficulty with the response format can also explain a delay. Self-report is informative but not direct access to all mental processes.

**A possible test.** Ask whether reported thought changes across matched task contexts, accounting for task difficulty and reporting demands. The project has not collected these measurements.

**Evidence boundary.** Strawson et al. studied reported ongoing thought in an N-back task. This is relevant to state/context questions; it does not establish constant complex problem-solving.

**Selected sources:** [Strawson et al. (2024)](REFERENCES.md#ongoing) · [Murray, Lesser & Lawson (2005)](REFERENCES.md#attention)

## 03 / Depth and switching of attention

**State & attention · Related theoretical work**

> Does maintaining attention differ from moving attention between activities?

**Originating observation.** Masiha described intense focus and a possible distinction between internal and externally responsive states.

**Interpretation.** Separate persistence, breadth, voluntary switching and switching cost. A proposed two-state account must compete with continuous or multi-state alternatives.

**What to measure.** Matched continuation and switch trials; task accuracy or response time as appropriate; interest, practice, fatigue and task difficulty recorded independently.

**Competing explanation.** An apparent switching effect may reflect unequal task demands or interest rather than a distinct attention mechanism.

**A possible test.** Prespecify a contrast between continuation and switching. Compare discrete-state and continuous explanations only if sample size and measurement reliability support that comparison.

**Evidence boundary.** Monotropism provides a related theoretical account of attention allocation. Conceptual overlap does not validate this project’s particular state model.

**Selected sources:** [Murray, Lesser & Lawson (2005)](REFERENCES.md#attention) · [Strawson et al. (2024)](REFERENCES.md#ongoing)

## 04 / Sensory regulation and agency

**Body & regulation · Exploratory extension**

> Which properties of sensory situations matter: intensity, predictability, preference or control?

**Originating observation.** Water, music, pressure, movement and attention to sensation appeared together in Masiha’s questions about bodily experience.

**Interpretation.** Do not merge these into one universal sensory-load number. Physical intensity, personal preference, predictability and the ability to stop an experience are different variables.

**What to measure.** Specify one sensory dimension and a participant-selected outcome. Separate physical stimulus measurements, reported experience and observed behaviour.

**Competing explanation.** Pain, familiarity, expectation, choice and previous experience can affect responses. Behaviour alone cannot identify the relevant sensory dimension.

**A possible test.** Begin with participant-led, non-distressing observation. Only consider controlled contrasts after suitable co-design, ethics review and an accessible stopping procedure.

**Evidence boundary.** Selected sensory/food work and guidance provide background. They do not validate a single regulatory mechanism for all stimuli or people.

**Selected sources:** [Chistol et al. (2018)](REFERENCES.md#sensory) · [NICE (CG170)](REFERENCES.md#nice)

## 05 / Taste, texture and food choice

**Body & regulation · Related observational work**

> Does a food preference reflect taste alone, or a combination of sensory and contextual properties?

**Originating observation.** Masiha reported noticing preferences for salty, sour or protein-containing foods in some observations. Their frequency and representativeness have not been established.

**Interpretation.** Separate flavour, texture, smell, temperature, familiarity and availability. A food category bundles several properties and cannot identify which one explains a choice.

**What to measure.** Operationally defined food refusal, variety and sensory features. Record access, routines and relevant physical factors rather than assuming a sensory cause.

**Competing explanation.** Health, familiarity, availability or habit may explain the same pattern. Protein content and sensory qualities are not interchangeable variables.

**A possible test.** A future observational design could assess whether independently coded food properties relate to choices. No diet or feeding intervention is proposed here.

**Evidence boundary.** Bandini and Chistol support studying food selectivity and sensory associations. Neither establishes a universal preference for salt, sourness or protein.

**Selected sources:** [Bandini et al. (2010)](REFERENCES.md#food) · [Chistol et al. (2018)](REFERENCES.md#sensory)

## 06 / Repetition and its possible function

**Body & regulation · Related qualitative work**

> What purpose does a repetitive action serve for the person performing it?

**Originating observation.** Masiha’s questions connect repetition, rhythm, movement, comfort and concentration.

**Interpretation.** Distinguish the visible form of an action from its reported function. Calmness, sustained attention and task performance are separate outcomes.

**What to measure.** Accessible first-person accounts and context-specific descriptions; use participant-selected outcomes rather than judging an action only by its appearance.

**Competing explanation.** An action may have multiple functions or vary in meaning across situations. Predictable rhythm alone does not prove a calming effect.

**A possible test.** Start by documenting reported function and context. Any subsequent within-person comparison should preserve choice and avoid forcing suppression of harmless self-regulation.

**Evidence boundary.** Kapp et al. report autistic adults’ experiences. Qualitative accounts inform the question but do not prove a universal causal effect of a specific stimulus.

**Selected sources:** [Kapp et al. (2019)](REFERENCES.md#stimming)

## 07 / Sleep and unfinished activity

**Body & regulation · Unsubstantiated causal interpretation**

> Could involvement in an unfinished activity be associated with delayed sleep in some situations?

**Originating observation.** Masiha wondered whether apparent resistance to sleep might sometimes reflect unfinished thinking or activity.

**Interpretation.** Keep this as a narrow question, not an explanation of autistic sleep differences. Association between thinking and wakefulness does not identify causal direction.

**What to measure.** If justified, use a non-interventional record of sleep timing, routines, context and self-reported activity, with appropriate professional oversight.

**Competing explanation.** Sleep schedules, physical discomfort, anxiety, light, medication or other factors may be relevant. Being awake can also create more time for thinking.

**A possible test.** First assess whether the proposed construct can be recorded reliably. Do not reduce sleep or induce distress to test it.

**Evidence boundary.** NICE discusses multifactorial assessment of sleep difficulties. It is not evidence for the unfinished-problem explanation.

**Selected sources:** [NICE (CG170)](REFERENCES.md#nice)

## 08 / Feeling, expression and interpretation

**Communication & measurement · Conceptual distinction**

> When a message is not understood, where in the exchange did information become unavailable?

**Originating observation.** Masiha’s account includes emotion, frustration, tolerance and the effort required for people to understand one another.

**Interpretation.** Distinguish feeling, recognizing a feeling, naming it, expressing it and being understood. An observer cannot infer the absence of emotion from a limited outward response.

**What to measure.** A participant’s preferred communication method; their confirmation of intended meaning; listener interpretation. Do not assign an unobserved emotion as ground truth.

**Competing explanation.** Misunderstanding can arise from the request, listener expectations, context or reporting method. A communication task is not a direct measure of emotional experience.

**A possible test.** With consent, compare agreement about intended meaning across accessible response formats while keeping demands as comparable as possible.

**Evidence boundary.** Information-transfer studies are adjacent evidence, not direct tests of feeling. The 2025 replication limits a simple reading of the 2020 group-composition result.

**Selected sources:** [Crompton et al. (2020)](REFERENCES.md#communication) · [Crompton et al. (2025)](REFERENCES.md#replication) · [NICE (CG170)](REFERENCES.md#nice)

## 09 / Ability and the response interface

**Communication & measurement · Primary study candidate**

> How much does the required response format affect the performance we observe?

**Originating observation.** Masiha used a computer-without-a-monitor metaphor to question whether limited visible output necessarily means limited underlying ability.

**Interpretation.** The useful point is measurement dependence. The metaphor’s limit is equally important: a person’s unobserved capacity is not known in advance, and cannot be reconstructed from silence alone.

**What to measure.** A prespecified task outcome using response formats whose construct validity, difficulty, language and motor demands have been examined.

**Competing explanation.** Different tests may measure different constructs. A performance difference may reflect difficulty, practice or instruction rather than hidden ability.

**A possible test.** Co-design matched task versions, counterbalance order and estimate within-person differences. Test whether interface variables improve out-of-sample prediction.

**Evidence boundary.** Dawson et al. found differences across intelligence tests in their samples. This does not establish hidden exceptional ability in every person or equivalence of all instruments.

**Selected sources:** [Dawson et al. (2007)](REFERENCES.md#cognition)

## 10 / Family traits and joint prediction

**Family & context · Exploratory extension**

> Does measured family information add reproducible information beyond an individual-only model?

**Originating observation.** Masiha noticed possible similarities in attention, regulation and flexibility among relatives, and wanted to consider the family as part of the observational frame.

**Interpretation.** Measure traits instead of assigning blame or assumed parental contributions. The relevant test is incremental prediction, with careful handling of shared causes and reporting effects.

**What to measure.** Comparable trait measures, multiple informants where feasible, relevant shared-environment variables and explicit family identifiers for data splitting.

**Competing explanation.** Shared environment, common reporters, recruitment, population structure or correlated measurement errors can produce apparent family associations.

**A possible test.** Compare individual-parent predictors with a joint model on held-out families. Treat interaction terms and information-theoretic synergy as different constructs.

**Evidence boundary.** Broad-autism-phenotype research makes family traits a legitimate topic. It does not validate this project’s synergy metric or fixed maternal/paternal percentages.

**Selected sources:** [Sasson et al. (2013)](REFERENCES.md#family)

## 11 / Person–environment fit

**Family & context · Primary study candidate**

> Does a defined change in context alter a defined outcome for the same person?

**Originating observation.** Masiha used an analogy of a system operating under different conditions to question judgments made without knowing the setting.

**Interpretation.** Translate the analogy into a specific environmental demand and a meaningful outcome. A numerical mismatch is only a model definition until validated.

**What to measure.** One prespecified context variable, accessible participant-selected outcomes, task demands and time-varying confounders.

**Competing explanation.** Practice, fatigue, order effects, physical state and changes in instruction may explain observed differences.

**A possible test.** Use repeated observations and matched conditions. Separate the main context comparison from exploratory explanations of why it occurs.

**Evidence boundary.** Environmental considerations in guidance support asking contextual questions. They do not validate a universal distance metric or explain away all disability.

**Selected sources:** [NICE (CG170)](REFERENCES.md#nice)

## 12 / First-person experience and inference

**Foundation · Philosophical boundary**

> What can observation tell us about an experience that is available primarily to the person having it?

**Originating observation.** Masiha asked whether observers may miss aspects of internal experience, awareness or the felt passage of time.

**Interpretation.** Use this question to mark epistemic limits. First-person report, measured performance and a third party’s interpretation are different kinds of evidence.

**What to measure.** Only operationally defined, accessible reports or observable outcomes can enter the empirical proposal. Unavailable reports should remain unavailable, not be filled in by narrative.

**Competing explanation.** An evocative account may be impossible to distinguish from rival accounts if it makes no different observable predictions.

**A possible test.** State what an interpretation would predict and what would challenge it. Claims without a workable empirical distinction remain philosophical questions.

**Evidence boundary.** No claim about ultimate consciousness, mind-reading or an altered-state treatment is supported or proposed.

**Selected sources:** No direct supporting source assigned in the current register.

## 13 / Phenotype geometry and label-free discovery

**Modelling · Future analysis, not implemented**

> Can a carefully measured feature space reveal stable structure without imposing diagnostic categories?

**Originating observation.** Masiha wants mathematics and AI to connect the ideas rather than reduce people to a single visible behaviour.

**Interpretation.** Define features and their measurement properties before considering a latent-space map. The axes, distances and clusters depend on the data representation and algorithm.

**What to measure.** Feature provenance, scale, reliability and missingness; model specification; resampling stability; performance in an independent cohort.

**Competing explanation.** Scaling, sampling, nuisance variables or dimension-reduction choices can create apparent structure. A cluster is not automatically a biological type.

**A possible test.** Compare multiple defensible representations, investigate confounding and assess stability. Keep discovery exploratory and use independent evaluation for subsequent claims.

**Evidence boundary.** No participant feature space, trained AI model, validated family-synergy estimator or diagnostic embedding exists in this project.

**Selected sources:** No direct supporting source assigned in the current register.

## 14 / The integrated proposal

**Modelling · Untested synthesis**

> Does the combined framework offer anything beyond simpler, existing explanations?

**Originating observation.** Masiha wants the connections between the topics to be considered together, while preserving the meaning of each observation.

**Interpretation.** The proposed contribution is an explicit, traceable organization of hypotheses. Integration becomes an empirical contribution only if a defined model yields useful, reproducible results.

**What to measure.** A strong baseline, candidate model, primary outcome, predefined improvement criterion and held-out evaluation.

**Competing explanation.** The framework may repackage existing concepts or add flexible variables that fit the original sample without generalizing.

**A possible test.** Prioritize one narrow comparison. Revise or reject it if the improvement is negligible, instrument-specific, confounded or not reproducible.

**Evidence boundary.** Novelty is not established by this dossier. A systematic literature review and expert critique are needed before claiming a new theory.
