# Selected source register

[← Project](../README.md) · [Claim atlas](CLAIM-ATLAS.md)

These 11 entries reproduce the application’s selective register. Summaries are project interpretations, not quotations, independent reappraisals or a systematic review. A source relevant to one component does not validate the synthesis. Read the original publication before relying on its findings.

<a id="who"></a>

## World Health Organization · 2025

[Autism](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/autism-spectrum-disorders)

**Type:** Background

**Project summary:** Abilities and support needs vary across people.

**Limit:** General background, not a test of the proposed framework.

---

<a id="attention"></a>

## Murray, Lesser & Lawson · 2005

[Attention, monotropism and the diagnostic criteria for autism](https://monotropism.org/murray-lesser-lawson/)

**Type:** Theory

**Project summary:** Proposes an account centred on allocation of attention.

**Limit:** Theoretical relevance does not establish a two-state model or reveal thought content.

---

<a id="ongoing"></a>

## Strawson et al. · 2024

[Differences in ongoing thought between autistic and non-autistic adults](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-78286-6)

**Type:** Task / experience sampling

**Project summary:** Reported thought modality varied less between easy and difficult task contexts in the autistic group.

**Limit:** Task-specific self-report does not demonstrate constant complex thought or direct access to mental content.

---

<a id="food"></a>

## Bandini et al. · 2010

[Food selectivity in children with autism spectrum disorders and typically developing children](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20362301/)

**Type:** Observational

**Project summary:** The study found group differences in food selectivity in its sample.

**Limit:** It does not establish universal salty, sour or protein preferences or their causes.

---

<a id="sensory"></a>

## Chistol et al. · 2018

[Sensory sensitivity and food selectivity in children with autism spectrum disorder](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6215327/)

**Type:** Observational

**Project summary:** Oral sensory sensitivity was associated with food refusal in the sample.

**Limit:** Association does not identify causation or a universal sensory mechanism.

---

<a id="stimming"></a>

## Kapp et al. · 2019

[“People should be allowed to do what they like”: Autistic adults’ views and experiences of stimming](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30818970/)

**Type:** Qualitative

**Project summary:** Participants described experiences and regulatory functions of stimming.

**Limit:** Qualitative accounts are not a causal test of a particular stimulus or evidence of uniform effects.

---

<a id="nice"></a>

## NICE · CG170

[Autism spectrum disorder in under 19s: support and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg170/chapter/Recommendations)

**Type:** Guidance

**Project summary:** Addresses individualized considerations including communication, environment, sleep and feeding.

**Limit:** Clinical guidance is not evidence for the unfinished-thought hypothesis or this project’s model.

---

<a id="cognition"></a>

## Dawson et al. · 2007

[The level and nature of autistic intelligence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17680932/)

**Type:** Comparison of instruments

**Project summary:** Performance differed across Raven and Wechsler tests in the studied samples.

**Limit:** Test differences do not establish exceptional hidden ability in everyone or equivalence of constructs.

---

<a id="family"></a>

## Sasson et al. · 2013

[Autism and the broad autism phenotype: familial patterns and intergenerational transmission](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23639131/)

**Type:** Family trait study

**Project summary:** Examines broad-autism-phenotype traits and familial patterns.

**Limit:** Does not establish parental blame, fixed parental percentages or the proposed synergy measure.

---

<a id="communication"></a>

## Crompton et al. · 2020

[Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32431157/)

**Type:** Information-transfer experiment

**Project summary:** The experiment supported effective within-group autistic information transfer and a role for group composition.

**Limit:** A particular task is not a complete account of communication or emotion; consider the later replication.

---

<a id="replication"></a>

## Crompton et al. · 2025

[Information transfer within and between autistic and non-autistic people](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02163-z)

**Type:** Registered Report

**Project summary:** With 311 participants, no difference in information transfer was found between single-neurotype and mixed-neurotype chains.

**Limit:** This contrasts with the earlier result. It limits a simple generalization, without resolving every communication context.
