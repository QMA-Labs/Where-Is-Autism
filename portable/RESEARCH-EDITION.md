# Research-group edition — design and information architecture

Audience: a university research group evaluating an unsolicited early-stage research inquiry. The first screen must answer: what is the question, what currently exists, what would be tested, and what help is requested?

All content is English. Four reading routes: Overview; Ideas & hypotheses; Evidence; Proposed study. Creator and contact information follow the argument and also appear in the footer. No boot sequence, tutorial sequence, animated demonstrations or diagnostic language. Interactivity supports retrieval: claim selection, evidence cross-links, reviewer questions and document exports.

Visual direction: quiet near-black editorial canvas, warm-white serif headings, restrained ice-blue links, thin rules, generous margins, readable body typography. Static model schematic with explicit conceptual status. System fonts, no remote resources. Light printable document generated from the same content. The full dossier and a short research inquiry are standalone offline HTML documents.

Content priorities: preserve all fourteen topics, but separate a tractable primary study from exploratory extensions and philosophical questions. Every hypothesis has an interpretation, operationalization, rival account, proposed test and evidence boundary. Explain 'distributed' as distribution of relevant observations across measurement contexts; do not imply autism resides in relatives or that a single framework is established. Explicitly distinguish prior literature from this project's untested synthesis.

Completion criteria: four working routes; English throughout UI and exported documents; verified creator contacts; 14 detailed entries and 11 linked sources; evidence-to-claim mapping; a feasible staged proposal with unresolved design decisions; no invented results or institutional endorsement; offline, keyboard and responsive checks; no automatic external requests. No submission is sent to any institution.
